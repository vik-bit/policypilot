## 3. What the numbers show

All 6 regulations (25 labelled requirements, 27 affected clauses) completed on Groq's free tier
in about 17 minutes.

- **Search finds the clauses; the agent adds judgement.** Recall rises a little (89% → 93%), but the
  big gains are precision (85% → **100%**, no unrelated clause flagged) and impact labels
  (50% → **96%**).
- **The misses, read by hand:**
  - `reg-03 R4` (access reviews): the agent flagged the user-access clause `info-security§4.1` but not
    the privileged-access clause `§4.2`. A genuine miss.
  - `reg-04 R2` (consent for tokenisation): it chose the consent clause `data-privacy§2.1`, which the
    labels accept as an alternative, instead of the card-storage clause `info-security§3.2`.
  - `reg-03 R5` (vendor audits): labelled *missing from policy* where the human label is *needs change*.
    The policy has an annual questionnaire rather than an audit, so both readings are defensible.

### A bug the evaluation caught

The one unverified quote (53/54) was **real**: the model copied the sentence exactly but typed
"board‑approved" with a Unicode non-breaking hyphen (U+2011), and the fact-checker only normalised
en and em dashes. It rejected a correct quote three times, so the finding reached the reviewer flagged
"check manually". That's the safe failure direction (nothing false was shown as verified), but it's
still a bug. The checker now normalises every Unicode hyphen and space variant, with a regression test
(`tests/test_verifier.py`), and the same quote verifies on a re-check. The scores above are left as
measured.
