Regulatory change management

Know exactly which policies a new regulation affects — in minutes, not weeks.

PolicyPilot reads a new regulation, finds every clause in your company policies that it touches, explains the impact in plain English and drafts the updated wording. Every quote it relies on is checked word-for-word against the source, and nothing changes until a person approves it.

How it works

  1. 1. Read the rule

    The new regulation is split into its individual requirements (R1, R2, …).

  2. 2. Find affected clauses

    Each requirement is matched against every policy clause — by exact words and by meaning.

  3. 3. Assess & draft

    Each match is classified and new wording is drafted where something must change.

  4. 4. Verify the evidence

    Every quote is checked against the original text. Anything unproven is sent back or flagged.

  5. 5. You decide

    Review each change side by side, then approve or reject it and export the report.

What the results mean

Needs change

The policy says something the new rule no longer allows — e.g. it keeps data for 10 years, but the rule now says 3.

Missing from policy

Already compliant

    Quick start

    Your first report takes about a minute.

    1. 1

      Open New analysis

      Use the menu on the left. You'll see a list of sample regulations from a fictional regulator.

    2. 2

      Pick a regulation

      Click any row. A preview on the right lists the requirements it contains, so you know what will be checked. To use your own text, switch to Paste your own.

    3. 3

      Click Run analysis

      A progress panel shows each stage as it happens: reading, searching, assessing, verifying. You don't need to do anything while it runs.

    4. 4

      Review the report

      It opens automatically. Start with the summary at the top, then work through each requirement and approve or reject the suggested changes.

    Reviewing & exporting

    • Approve or Reject each suggestion. Click the same button again to undo. Decisions are saved automatically.
    • The review progress bar at the top of the report shows how many items are left.
    • Use the filters (All, Needs change, Missing, Compliant, Check manually) to focus on what matters first.
    • Export downloads the full report — summary, findings, evidence and your decisions — as a document you can share or attach to an email.

    Using your own documents

    Policies: go to Policy library and click Upload a policy. Word-processed policies saved as .pdf, .txt or .md all work. Numbered headings such as “3.2 Data Retention” give the best results, because each numbered section becomes one clause that can be cited.

    Regulations: on New analysis, choose Paste your own and paste the text.

    Documents stay on the computer running PolicyPilot. In AI mode, the regulation and the relevant clauses are sent to the AI provider to be analysed.

    Demo mode vs AI mode

    Demo mode

    Runs when no AI key is set up. Uses the same search and fact-checking, but a simple rule-based assessment instead of AI. Good for trying the product. Status labels and suggested wording are rough.

    AI mode

    An AI model reads each clause, decides what needs to change, explains why, and drafts proper new wording. Its quotes are still fact-checked, and it must fix any that fail.

    To switch on AI mode (for whoever set PolicyPilot up): create a file called .env in the project folder with one of:

    • Groq (has a free tier): PP_PROVIDER=groq and GROQ_API_KEY=your-key
    • Claude: PP_PROVIDER=claude and ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=your-key

    Then restart the app. The status card in the menu turns green and shows which model is running.

    Questions

    Can I trust the suggested wording?

    Treat it as a strong first draft. The evidence badge proves the quotes are real, but not that the interpretation is right — that's why every change needs a person's approval.

    Why does it say “Check manually” on some items?

    The system couldn't confirm a quote word-for-word, even after asking the AI to correct it. The finding may still be useful, but read the original clause before approving.

    A clause I expected is missing. Why?

    Search may not have ranked it highly enough. Use the Search playground to see how it ranks, and check the clause exists in the Policy library.

    Is this legal advice?

    No. PolicyPilot is a drafting and review aid. The sample documents describe a fictional company and regulator.
